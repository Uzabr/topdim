# sizbiz sales-manager playbook

This is an internal guide. It does not replace approved pricing, contracts,
legal advice, or a formal commercial proposal.

## Sales objective

Find businesses suited to the coupon model, understand their need, explain
sizbiz honestly, and hand a qualified partner to operational onboarding.

## Best-fit partners

- local businesses with a clear product or service;
- businesses able to fulfill the published promise;
- businesses with sufficient inventory or service capacity;
- teams seeking new and repeat customers;
- partners ready to train staff on QR/PIN redemption;
- owners able to approve price, validity, and limits.

Potential segments include food service, non-medical personal services,
entertainment, learning, retail, and local services, subject to product and
legal policy.

## Poor fit

- cannot deliver the promised quality or volume;
- relies on hidden charges;
- staff refuses to accept coupons;
- demands guaranteed sales;
- offer violates law, consumer rights, or platform policy;
- no accountable approver or support contact.

## Before contact

Record the business name, category, locations, contact role, current marketing
channels, likely need, lead source, and known risks.

## Discovery questions

1. Which business problem are you trying to solve?
2. Who is your typical customer?
3. Which offer is valuable to them and sustainable for you?
4. When do you need additional demand?
5. What quantity, branch, or time limits apply?
6. Who approves the final terms?
7. Who will accept QR/PIN at the location?
8. How do you currently measure advertising?
9. What could prevent successful fulfillment?
10. Which next step are you ready to take?

## 30-second pitch

> sizbiz helps local businesses turn a promotion into a managed digital
> process. We help prepare the offer, show it to buyers, and let authorized
> staff redeem purchased coupons by QR or PIN. The owner can see sales and
> redemptions, and terms are approved before publication.

## Demo sequence

1. Open `sizbiz.uz` and find an offer.
2. Show price, benefit, validity, and conditions.
3. Explain cart, order, and the buyer coupon.
4. Show partner request and approval.
5. Show QR/PIN redemption and history.
6. Show available partner statistics.
7. State current limitations.
8. Agree on owner and next step.

Do not perform a real financial transaction to imply production acquiring:
payment is currently in demo mode.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “A discount reduces margin” | Design a sustainable offer and limits first; sizbiz should not dictate a loss-making mechanic. |
| “Staff will forget” | Assign an owner and train cashiers on the short QR/PIN process before launch. |
| “Do you guarantee sales?” | No. We provide a channel and measurable process; demand depends on offer, audience, and execution. |
| “Can we publish immediately?” | The card is prepared and approved to protect both sides. |
| “We already use Instagram/Telegram” | sizbiz adds purchase, redemption, and result tracking to reach. |
| “When will feature X launch?” | State only the approved current status and forward roadmap requests. |

## What sales may promise

- a digital offer card after the preparation process;
- partner approval before publication;
- access to supported offer and staff management;
- QR/PIN redemption for authorized staff context;
- an operational escalation path.

## What sales must not promise without approval

- specific sales, income, or ROI;
- category exclusivity;
- a price, commission, or payout schedule;
- a feature-delivery date;
- legal, tax, or financial compliance;
- production integration with a named payment provider;
- unconditional refunds in every situation.

## Qualification and handoff

A qualified lead includes a confirmed need, decision-maker, draft offer,
locations and constraints, cashier owner, risks, open questions, and agreed
next step. Sales then hands it to onboarding without adding undocumented
promises.

## Sales quality checklist

- lead record and source are current;
- status and next owner are clear;
- next contact has a date;
- limitations were disclosed;
- consent was explicit;
- personal and commercial data goes only to authorized staff.
